Guillotine Reels

Dark Fantasy Slot Game

Beta — Personal Game Design Project

Guillotine Reels is a dark fantasy slot game built around a non-standard board and a payline system designed specifically for it. The design is built around anticipation — probability, pacing, animation and audio are tuned together so every spin raises the question of what happens next, and every reward lands with weight. The current build is a playable beta, shaped by continuous iteration on how each system feels to play.

Design, systems and direction by Jon Arbe — prototyped and built with AI-assisted development.

About

Jon Arbe

Who I Am

I’ve always been interested in the way things make us feel. A film can make you uncomfortable without telling you why. A song can change your mood in a few seconds. A video can make you laugh, surprise you, or remind you of something. I naturally pay attention to those details.

That’s what I want to bring into game design: thinking not only about what the player does, but about what the player feels while doing it. I want to design experiences around emotions.

What I Design

Areas I’m interested in and actively developing as a designer — not a claim of years of professional experience in each one. Click any of them for a short note on what they mean to me.

  • How the moment-to-moment actions of a game work — what the player actually does, and why doing it should feel good on its own, before any reward shows up.

  • How individual mechanics connect into something bigger, so the board feels like one connected space where things happen — not a set of isolated pieces sitting next to each other.

  • Designing around what the player feels, notices and anticipates in the moment — not just what they click, but what they understand and expect right after.

  • The texture of an interaction — timing, weight, feedback. The difference between a mechanic that technically works and one that feels right in the hand.

  • Deciding when things happen and, just as important, when they don’t — giving moments room to breathe instead of making everything equally intense.

  • How wins, near-misses and surprises are presented — shaping anticipation and satisfaction, not only the numbers sitting behind them.

  • Thinking about uncertainty, anticipation and small frustrations — and how they can keep someone curious instead of pushing them away.

  • Turning an idea into something playable as fast as possible, so I can feel whether it works instead of just imagining that it does.

  • Being willing to rebuild something that doesn’t feel right, even after it’s already built — and knowing the difference between “needs polish” and “needs to change.”

Design Philosophy

“Know what you want to communicate, and be as clean as possible with it.”

First, I need to understand what I want the player to feel. Then I try to remove everything that doesn’t help communicate that feeling.

I believe my life experiences have given me a strong perception of emotions, and I try to use that when designing experiences for other people.
