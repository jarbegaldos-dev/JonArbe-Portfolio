01 · The idea

A world, a machine, a feeling.

Ossuary Reels is my independent game-design project: a dark-fantasy slot built around a ribcage-shaped board.

The design challenge is to make an unusual shape playable, give its mechanics a shared visual language and use animation and sound to make each event readable and expressive.

This case study follows three threads: art direction, mechanics and game feel. It documents work in progress; the final build, artwork and validation will replace the current development material.

03 · The board

A ribcage instead of a grid

The board is a ribcage of independent reels: a top rib, a centre spine and four ribs on each side, with the chest at the bottom. I designed the paylines around the bones, so a win can travel in directions a 5×3 grid never allows.

Goal
A distinctive board that still reads at a glance.
Decision
Keep the ribcage layout, but build it as a working machine with clear lanes.
Rule
Lines start on the left and may step one row up or down per reel: 173 paths, three of a kind or wild to pay.
04 · Mechanics

How the mechanics connect

Cuts link the base reels to the chest, the Raven chain and the bonus modes.

Raven Chain

Perch
Cut a Raven WILD and a raven perches above the reels. Ravens on the same side add to their perch multiplier.
Stone throw
A skeleton can throw a stone after a cut. If it hits, the raven falls through its column and leaves multiplier WILDs in the cells it crosses.
Free spin
Each raven hit grants a Raven Spin. The stone becomes less likely to hit as the chain progresses.

I kept the hold between the door opening and the throw so the player has time to look from the skeleton to the perched raven.

Blades & the Chest

Fill the Chest
Only cut Shackles advance the Chest counter. A cash bag attached to Shackles pays separately when the cut bag reaches the Chest.
Open it
Once the target is reached, the next base spin paying more than five times the bet opens the Chest.
Choose
Collect the displayed prize or spin the wheel. The wheel can award a multiplier or open Execution or The Judgment.

Guillotine trial

Execution Bonus

Enter
In the base game, three initial Mini Blades without the upper Guillotine can open Execution. It can also start from the Guillotine trial, the Chest wheel or Buy Bonus.
Cut
Condemned skeletons lock into the side stocks. The Guillotine pays for hands and restores three chances. The Scythe pays for heads and leaves the skeletons available for a later hand cut.
Build the multiplier
Cut hands fill the box. Crossing its thresholds raises the multiplier on later cuts. Any skeleton still uncut when the bonus ends goes unpaid.

Blood Mark: A chained skeleton can flash BLIGHTED before a cut. Its multiplier transfers to the Guillotine and boosts that cut. Last Breath can grant another chance when the bonus would otherwise end.

The Judgment Bonus

Enter
In the base game, three initial Mini Blades with the upper Guillotine can open The Judgment. The Chest wheel and Buy Bonus offer other routes.
Bells
Each spin uses a bell. A crow landing in the middle of a nest reel can earn another bell when Death reaps it.
Lantern
Condemned that land in stocks add their soul value to the lantern. Blighted figures add an enhanced value and strengthen the scythe.
Sentence
The scythe grows as Death reaps crows. At the Sentence, the scythe multiplier applies to the final lantern total.

Max Win

MAX lands on reel 2 and WIN on reel 4. Their landing reveals the hooded companion on reel 3 and triggers the top prize.

05 · Feel & sound

Let moments breathe

Not everything needs to happen at once, and not every moment needs the same intensity. Rhythm, pauses, sound and animation decide how much weight a moment has. A lot of things worked technically and still didn’t feel right, so I kept going back.

04

A big win, cut to the music

A big win should feel like the climax of a song, not a number going up.

The problem

A ×40 win took 21 seconds with the spin button locked, and small wins felt heavy.

What I changed: the count depends on the win

up to ×25
1–2 s
up to ×100
4.2 s
up to ×250
8.2 s · 2 bars
up to ×500
12.2 s · 3 bars
×500 and up
~18 s · the full show

Small wins count fast and silent. Above ×25 the music starts with the counter, and the number always lands on a cymbal hit. I measured the hits in the track by hand (4.205, 8.185 and 12.205 s) and the number lands 30 ms after each one. In turbo the musical tiers don’t speed up: they use half the bars, so they stay in time.

The raven lands on the middle digit once the count settles.

06 · Iteration

Evolution & redesigns

Most of the development was iteration. At one point I threw away almost the entire original reel structure because, although it worked, it didn’t feel right. It was a big step back, and one of the best decisions of the project.

It kept happening: I’d improve one part and suddenly another felt weaker. I started calling them mountains.

Improve one, find the next one that’s weaker, go back, cut what isn’t helping, test again.

From the dev log · 244 logged changes since June 2026

  1. Jul 9

    Paylines. Chose a network of 173 paths that follow the bones, instead of fixed lines drawn on a grid.

  2. Jul 12

    Big-win counter. Rebuilt from scratch after a power cut wiped the work in progress.

  3. Oct 7

    Winning symbols. Cut down to a single shake and a single flash.

  4. Oct 9

    Raven chain. A dead raven now grants a free spin and the stone’s odds step down spin by spin. Golden crows added. The big-win count re-timed by win size.

  5. Oct 13

    Anticipation pass. The board darkens and the ravens tremble while the stone flies; decisive respins hold back before they stop.

  6. Oct 14

    New HUD. An hourglass spin button and a clover menu, built to be reused in future slots; a summary screen after every bonus; motion blur on the spin.

What I learned: a mechanic can work perfectly and still not feel right, and designing a game you enjoy even when you lose is much harder than it sounds. How a loss, a near-miss or an almost-reward is presented changes everything.
